01 — The idea

You don't need more followers. You need a bunch.

Bunchy is not a feed, a dating app, or a place to accumulate an audience. It introduces you to a small number of compatible people and gets out of the way. The identity has to carry that: warm and social enough to feel like somewhere you'd want to be, disciplined enough to be trusted with who you are.

Belonging, shown structurally

Four shapes, four sizes, four angles, one cluster. Individuality is the size difference; belonging is the even gap.

Warm, never clinical

Cream instead of white, navy instead of black. The ground does the friendliness so the accents don't have to shout.

Not the obvious vocabulary

No network nodes, no speech bubbles, no hearts, no handshakes, no silhouettes. Those say "social product", not this social product.

Not a generic AI startup

No sparkles, no gradient mesh, no corporate blue. Intelligence is signalled by one colour with one job, not by decoration.

Four shapes, one bunch

03 — The wordmark

Drawn, not set

The wordmark is vector paths, not live text in a licensed font. It renders identically on every machine, needs no font to load, and cannot be silently substituted. Monoline strokes with round caps: cap height 100, x-height from 28, descender to 116, stroke 20.

Coral
Knockout

The coral wordmark above is the one place coral appears as a letterform. Logotypes are explicitly exempt from WCAG contrast requirements — but the exemption stops at the logo, so coral never sets a headline, a link or a label anywhere else.

The hooked y is the signature

It is the one letter that could not have come from a stock face. It never gets straightened, and it never gets a second flourish elsewhere in the word.

Spacing is optical, not metric

The round c sits tighter to its neighbours than the flat-sided letters, and the diagonal of the y tighter still. Even gaps would look uneven.

Supporting typeface, for everything that isn't the logo

The product sets UI and marketing copy in a rounded geometric sans. Licence one of Nunito, Figtree or Poppins — all carry the roundness of the wordmark without competing with it. Until one is licensed and self-hosted, the product ships a tuned system stack: headings at 700 with -0.025em tracking, body at 400/1.6. Do not link a font from a CDN; self-host it, or the fallback will quietly replace the brand.

04 — Colour

One loud voice, three specialists

Coral is the only colour allowed to be loud, and it is the one people will remember. Purple, yellow and mint each hold exactly one meaning, which is what stops the palette from becoming decoration.

05 — Contrast

The rule that shapes the whole system

Every ratio below is measured, not estimated. One result decides more of this design than any other: white text on coral is 3.00:1, which fails WCAG AA for a button label. So Bunchy's primary button carries a deep navy label instead — 5.42:1, the signature colour completely unchanged.

Every accent carries a text-safe twin

The bright value is the fill. The darkened value — the "ink" — is the same hue pushed until it is legible as text on cream. Reach for the ink whenever the colour becomes a letterform.

Danger cannot also be red

When the primary action is coral, a bright red destructive button is a trap. Danger is #B3261E — deeper and browner, clearly not the brand — and destructive actions are rendered as tinted, not filled, so they never compete with the primary button for the eye.

07 — Misuse

How this breaks

Don't put white text on coral

3.00:1. The single most likely mistake, because it is what every other consumer app does.

Don't let the shapes touch or overlap

The gaps are the idea. Merged shapes read as one blob and the mark loses its meaning.

Don't recolour the cluster arbitrarily

Coral is always the largest shape. Full colour, single colour, or knockout — nothing else.

Don't rotate or rearrange the mark

The angles are already built in. Rotating the whole cluster makes it look like a mistake.

Don't use mint or yellow as text

1.71:1 and 1.47:1 on cream. They are fills. Their inks are what you write with.

Don't tint the page background

Cream is the ground everywhere. Coloured page backgrounds break the 60/25/10/5 balance instantly.

Don't add gradients to the mark

Flat colour only. A gradient makes it a crypto logo, and it will not survive a 16px favicon.

Don't spend coral on decoration

If everything is coral, nothing is the primary action. Repeated metadata stays neutral.
